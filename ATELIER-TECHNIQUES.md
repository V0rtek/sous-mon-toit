# Atelier des techniques (depuis le 26/11)

Ce que Justin découpe dans de vraies scènes, gardé **mot pour mot**, et les essais de variantes qu'on fait ensemble.
Règle (Justin, 26/11) : je ne fais pas les techniques à sa place. Il en parle ; moi je reformule de plusieurs façons et je
propose des variantes, **jamais le mot final** ; il dit ce qu'il en fait. Seul ce qu'il valide va dans `techniques.js`, dans ses mots.

## Comment ça s'imbrique (la chaîne)
```
l'histoire (le moteur : ce qui arrive, qui sait quoi — forcé par la logique)
  → l'intention d'un temps (ce que le public doit comprendre ou sentir à ce moment)
    → la technique (comment on le fait passer : le carnet de Justin)
      → les variantes (des répliques, des gestes, des silences précis)
        → le filtre du lecteur (ce que le format Last Call sait montrer : plans, silences, visages, bulles)
```
Ce qui est **forcé** : la structure (les causes, qui sait quoi, ce qui est planté puis payé). Ce qui est une **direction** : les
techniques (choisies selon l'intention, jamais imposées). Le lien entre les deux : l'intention (« ce que le public comprend »).

## Stranger's Pawn — le breakdown de Justin (26/11, gardé tel quel)

J = The main char

These techniques

### Dude 1 (he)
Its not even told that he lost the chess game but we understand. Example of exposition through emotional reactions.

Eyes a bit wide, talking fast, disbelief
- "Nah"
- "Nah-nah man"
- "You can't do that"
(Note, this could be interpretted as arrogance (ou mauvais perdant) if said in a slow way and without the disbelief face. Cest le genre de nuance que l'ia na pas toujours et devrait comprendre)
What the audience understands :
- He is in denial
- The ego of dude 1 is hurt
- He lost the game

J : "Another one?"
Silence while he's doing something (grabbing his bag = effort)
"Nah"
Show his hand in front of him (waiting for it to be shaken)
"I had enough losing for one day"
What the audiende understand:
- He lost multiple times
- He is actually a fair dude (handshake)
- He is about to go away, this will leave J alone at the table.
That's an example of a technique of relatability, this guy is human and have flaws and is multi-faced. All of that shown in the first 5 seconds.

J is smiling proud but not cruel by the whole time. He is still. And maintain eye contact. = he's confident.

At the end when the guy leaves. J looking at the dude going away, shaking his head "tss", big smile.
What we understand :
- thinking something like "oh this guy" in J head
- Happy to have won

J then focus on replacing his chess board, focus face

### Dude 2 (older, he)
- May I?

J interrupts focus, slightly curious look, look at the person.

J : "Please"

He slowly sits looking at J whole time.
What audience undertands :
- He has agenda? Showing that someone may have an agenda is important.
- He knows him?
- Dude is anxious?

J is still focusing on the board. Not done placing the chess parts.
J : You play often?

Dude : "eh I used to... its been a while"
A bit uncomfortable, dont know how to act, unfamiliar situation.

J unfocus the board and finally make eye contact with dude. Neutral and make gesture of "lets start playing".

Dude makes a chess move.

J : "So you live around here?" With relaxed neutral eye contact, said fast and confident.

Dude has a moment of silence looking at J "uh...".

Then looks at board focusing on question "I move here about..."

Takes a moment to recall. Perturbed (thinking about something else)

"I moved here about 25, 26 years ago"

Then another question from J : "Wow, long time. How do u like it here?"

He says no hesitation, and looking at J : "I grow tired of it sometimes"
Subtext/audience :
- Why that dark?
- Its like the subtext is about J. He's tired of J. Cool technique.
"But my life loves it"
- Ironical smile, subtext again (we learn later that J is having relationship with Dude wife and that dude is there to confirm/confront J, that's why he's so nervous and does all this)

Its dude time and he hasnt played yet (not focusing thing he's doing = technique?).
Q:
- Is he really here for chess?

Its about human performance that make us feel it is real. And through that exposition and different things.

Now disclaimer =
- of course all of that cannot be replicated in last call visual format.
- we may have to find alternatives

Skin in the game and tension is good. Uncomfortable / Competition / ...

What I want you to notice here is what scene agenda and realistic characters processing really is. It is constant, their state changes (offended, then calm down, then show hand) (leave because is tired of losing, been here for a while). Everything is said for a reason and is the output of the character inside world. We can see characters have multiple shits going on at once and also that they have different personalities.
